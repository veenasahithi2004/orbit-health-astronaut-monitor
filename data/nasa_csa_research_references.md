# Research references used for the prototype

These sources informed the feature choices and explanatory text. They do not validate this prototype or its example thresholds.

- NASA Human Research Program, [Five hazards of human spaceflight](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/): radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, and hostile or closed environments. NASA notes that hazards can interact.
- NASA Human Research Program, [Risks of human spaceflight](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/): includes cardiovascular adaptation, sensorimotor changes, immune responses, reduced fitness, bone changes, nutrition, and behavioral health risks.
- NASA Human Research Program, [Ongoing research](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/): examples include tracking wellbeing, activity, sleep, consistent physiological and psychological measures, immune biomarkers, and exercise countermeasures.
- Canadian Space Agency, [Monitoring radiation for astronaut health](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp): describes CANS, a compact autonomous instrument under development to measure neutron radiation exposure in space.
- Canadian Space Agency, [Why conduct science experiments in space?](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp): describes research to understand risks, develop countermeasures, and prepare for longer missions.

The app uses these as background only. Its sample data and guidance are fictional and illustrative, not NASA clinical guidance or a medical device.
